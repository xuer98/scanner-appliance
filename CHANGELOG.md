## v0.1.0 (2026-10-14)

- v0.1.0, the first release, on 2026-10-14, after three attempts on the same tag. The release action failed twice at its last step, patching every asset's metadata after upload, on this first non-prerelease tag; the publish step now uploads with the gh CLI, which creates the release as a draft, uploads over whatever a rerun finds there and publishes it. The container job then failed twice fetching modules for the engine tools, on HTTP/2 stream errors from the checksum database; those fetches now get four attempts. The third attempt was green end to end, with 38 signed assets, and the run's new last job handed the image set to the staging control plane, whose download page listed v0.1.0 three minutes after the release existed, with links that resolve. Found on 2026-10-15, from the run's log: the step that publishes the daemon release to the staging control plane printed `not set; skipping`, because the signing key exists only under its staging name and the step read the production one; it now falls back to `STAGING_RELEASE_KEY_PEM` as the bundle workflow does, for the next tag. The release is otherwise what the tag describes; manifest.json itself is not signed (the manifest is written after the signing pass), each artifact is.
- The tag also carries what landed between rc4 and it: the vendor download page on the control plane, fed by the release run; the gh-based release upload; and directives listed in creation order when their timestamps tie, which the Windows conformance test had hit.

## v0.1.0-rc4 (2026-10-12 to 2026-10-13)

- The release workflow, from 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-13, with that host registered as the `kvm` runner, a loopback-only dev control plane on it as the staging target and the feed snapshot published as a release asset (`FEED_TARBALL_URL`). Four tagged runs; the fourth, v0.1.0-rc4, was green in 65 minutes: binaries, the image with its OVA and zipped VHDX, the multi-arch container, the QEMU smoke against the staging control plane, the container smoke on GitHub's runner with the real feed (the engine ready with 95,104 scripts, a bundle to the canary group and its delta, a job, the depth operations, a parity report), keyless Sigstore signatures with certificates on every artifact, SBOMs for the binaries, the manifest with the container digest, and the GitHub prerelease with 38 assets. The three runs before it each found one thing: CMake under QEMU user emulation did not find libnet, libpcap, paho-mqtt and libcrypt in the linux/arm64 openvas stage although their packages were installed (install-openvas.sh now hands it the multiarch directory); redis refused to start in the emulated arm64 feed stage because its probe for an arm64 kernel copy-on-write bug misfires under emulation (the build-time pre-warm, which never forks, now ignores it); and the registry push targeted an organization that does not exist (the image follows the repository). The VHDX was also found to be 5.4 GB, over GitHub's 2 GiB asset limit, with under 1 GB of data in it; it is now converted with 1 MB blocks and shipped zipped at 0.8 GB.
- The published v0.1.0-rc4 itself, on 2026-10-13 on the same host: the qcow2 and the daemon downloaded from the GitHub release, checked against the manifest's digests and against their Sigstore certificates (the release workflow's identity at that tag), as was the container image's signature. That qcow2 booted under KVM against the staging control plane: enrolled with a heartbeat 25 s after power-on, the engine ready with every script within two and a half minutes. Then the daemon self-update under the unit itself, from the released daemon: a release that cannot start was back on the previous binary and reported as `rolled back after 3 failed starts` 45 s after it was published; one built with the wrong root CA, so that none of its heartbeats got through, was put back by the new daemon at the end of the 10-minute confirmation window and reported 10 min 48 s after publication; a good release was then confirmed by its own heartbeat 25 s after publication. The control plane held each failed release. The run found that a daemon that did update kept reporting the earlier failure in `update_error`; it now clears it when the new daemon starts, and `TestPhase3` covers it.
- The daily bundle workflow, on 2026-10-13, on the `kvm` runner against the staging control plane: that morning's Community Feed (202610130559) mirrored by `cp-api feed sync` in two minutes and cached under its version, nuclei built at the image's pin, the pinned templates checked out, a bundle of 105,555 files (95,104 scripts, 10,297 templates validated) built and signed in 67 s and published to the canary group in 57 s, five minutes in all. The released v0.1.0-rc4 image, booted under KVM as a canary with the image's 2026-10-09 feed, had the bundle applied and the engine on the new feed 2 min 14 s after publication, 31 s of it in the updating state; the same canary rule had already handed it the daemon release still in canary from the self-update run. The scheduled runs had failed at their preflight twice a day since 2026-10-10 for want of secrets with production names; the workflow now falls back to the staging secrets and runs where the `BUNDLE_RUNNER` variable says.

## 2026-10-09: the image under KVM

- Under KVM on 2026-10-09, on an Ubuntu 22.04 host with 4 vCPUs and 15 GB: the Packer build of the qcow2 from the Debian 12.15.0 netinst with that day's Community Feed (202610090603, 95,104 scripts; the VT cache pre-warm took 40 s and the whole build under 20 minutes, 887 MB compressed), then `ci/smoke/run-qemu.sh` in full on a BIOS boot and again on a UEFI boot through OVMF: enrollment within 10 to 30 s of power-on, the engine ready with every script, the console over serial, discovery and inventory of the appliance itself, discovery of the no-egress segment through lan0, directives, a configs-only bundle and its delta, and the pilot, depth and Qualys-replacement round trips. A daemon self-update under systemd: a release published to the canary group was fetched, swapped and confirmed by the new version's heartbeat 25 s later. The OVA and VHDX conversions of that image. The run fixed what it found: the preseed installed `systemd-resolved`, whose postinst breaks name resolution in the installer chroot, so grub-pc could not be fetched and the install stopped; harden.sh then installed the same package in the build VM, where resolved had no upstream server, so every later download failed (it now gets the build network's nameservers through a drop-in that cleanup.sh removes); the console test used `(?i)` patterns that Expect 5.45 never matches and left the Proxy screen with the wrong input; a bundle without a feed removed the seeded feed and waited for an empty feed version (it now keeps the installed feed); two smoke checks piped curl into `grep -q` under pipefail; Debian's `release/current` had moved to Debian 13, so the ISO now comes from the archive; and `make ova` looked for the qcow2 in a directory the template never writes.
